Getting started

This guide takes you from the box to live radar results in about 15 minutes.

What you need

  • Radar Base controller (ESP32-S3 board) and the 60 GHz radar module on its carrier board
  • 10 jumper wires (female to female) - included in the kit
  • Two USB cables: one for Radar Base, one to power the radar carrier
  • A computer with Chrome or Edge (Windows, macOS, Linux or ChromeOS)

1. Prepare the radar carrier

Remove all jumper caps from the carrier's serial header (J9) and from the P19 / GND / P12 boot header. Otherwise the carrier's own USB-serial chip competes with Radar Base on the same wires.

2. Wire it

Power everything off first. Connect GND first, then the rest. Labels are the ones printed on the radar carrier.

Radar Base pinRadar carrier labelPurpose
GNDGNDCommon ground (connect first)
GPIO17RX0Commands to the radar app
GPIO18TX0Results from the radar app
GPIO15RX1Loading apps into the radar
GPIO16TX1Replies while loading
GPIO4RSTRadar reset
GPIO5P12Boot select (also data select)
GPIO6P19Boot select
GPIO12SPI CLKRaw data clock (raw-data use only)
GPIO11SPI MOSIRaw data (do not use MISO)

The radar is powered by its own carrier (it draws up to about 600 mA). Never power it from the 3V3 pin of the controller board.

3. Power up in the right order

  1. Plug the controller's port marked USB into your computer. (The port marked UART is only for updating the controller's own firmware.)
  2. Then power the radar carrier.

Radar Base sets the radar's boot pins as soon as it starts, so the radar must come up second. If you powered them the other way round, open Diagnostics and press Restart the radar.

4. See it work

  1. Open the app in Chrome or Edge and press Connect USB; pick the port named "USB JTAG/serial".
  2. Choose Run a scenario → 3D presence & tracking.
  3. Walk in front of the radar. Your position appears as a blue dot.
